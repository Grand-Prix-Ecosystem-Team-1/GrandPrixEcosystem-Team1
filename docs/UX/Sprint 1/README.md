# Sprint 1 — UX Research, Ideation and Customer Form Design

## Overview

This folder contains the UX work completed during Sprint 1 for the Grand Prix Ecosystem project.

The sprint began with research into the overall customer requirement and internal data-management process. A team workshop was then conducted to explore problems with managing information in Monday.com.

While waiting for further client clarification, the team decided to focus on improving the customer requirement form. The current form and product requirements were analysed, form-specific HMW questions and ideas were developed, and a draft user flow and low-fidelity wireframes were created.

## Work completed

### 1. UX research

* Reviewed the product requirements and available client information.
* Identified the main users, needs and pain points.
* Documented research limitations and assumptions.
* Reviewed how customer requirements are currently collected and managed through Monday.com.

### 2. HMW and Crazy 8 workshop

* Prepared the workshop structure, activities and timings.
* Facilitated the HMW and Crazy 8 workshop.
* Recorded research insights and HMW questions.
* Collected and grouped the ideas generated by the team.
* Evaluated the workshop results and documented risks, dependencies and unanswered questions.

The workshop mainly focused on how internal teams find, organise and manage customer requirement data. It did not directly produce the customer form design.

### 3. Customer form design

The existing customer form was reviewed against the product requirements. The analysis found that technical terms such as EMAC are not explained, guidance is limited, required and optional information is unclear, and the form does not clearly support multiple locations and services. Customers also need a way to review and correct missing information before submitting, as incomplete submissions create additional follow-up work for the Grand Prix team.

The following HMW questions guided the design:

1. How might we help customers understand technical fields such as EMAC?
2. How might we help customers manage multiple locations and services without feeling overwhelmed?
3. How might we help customers identify and correct missing information before submission?

Ideas developed from these questions included a guided multi-step form, progress indicator, contextual `?` help, short field descriptions, collapsible location cards, service-specific questions, clear required-field labels, file-upload guidance, completion indicators and a final review page with descriptive validation errors.

A draft user flow was created:

1. Enter contact details.
2. Add one or more locations.
3. Complete the required details for each location.
4. Select and configure one or more services.
5. Review the complete request.
6. Correct missing or invalid information.
7. Submit the request.

Five low-fidelity wireframes were then created for contact details, location management, location details and EMAC help, service selection, and review and submission.

The user flow and wireframes demonstrate the proposed form experience but are not final. They require validation with Clint and the BA, particularly regarding EMAC guidance, mandatory fields and the client’s actual workflow.

## Current status

Sprint 1 research, ideation, form analysis, draft user flow and low-fidelity wireframes have been completed.

The customer form is the current design direction. However, the proposed flow and wireframes are based on the available product requirements and have not yet been validated with the client.

The following information remains to be confirmed:

* What EMAC means and where customers can find it.
* Which general contact fields are mandatory.
* Whether the proposed flow matches the client’s actual process.
* Whether the proposed interactions can be implemented within Monday.com.
* Whether any fields, instructions or service requirements are missing.

## Next steps

* Confirm the outstanding questions with Clint and the BA.
* Review the proposed flow with the team.
* Check technical feasibility with the developers.
* Update the user flow and wireframes using the feedback.
* Conduct usability testing with representative users.
* Develop a higher-fidelity form design after validation.
